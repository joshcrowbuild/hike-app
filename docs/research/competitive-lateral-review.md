# Competitive & Lateral-Inspiration Review — Adventure Planner

*Method note: 22 apps across three layers (direct competitors, lateral mechanic-donors, explicit anti-patterns) plus 5 cross-cutting themes, each claim adversarially fact-checked against primary sources. Verification date 2026-06-28. Where a source stayed unverifiable or a figure was corrected, the text hedges or re-attributes inline — read those hedges as load-bearing, not throat-clearing.*

**How to read this.** Section 2 (**Steal these first**) is the actionable core — a prioritized mechanics list tagged with effort and the rule/epic each maps to; if you read nothing else, read that and Section 3. Sections 4–5 are the evidence (per-app moves and gaps). Sections 6–8 are the guardrails: what never to build, how each invariant is served by an external exemplar, and where a tempting mechanic collides with an invariant. The single most important finding up front: **the entire field's dominant failure is epistemic — almost everyone shows undated, unsourced data as if it were current truth.** Best-in-class provenance exists only at the *feature* level (FarOut's Water Status), never the *app* level. Owning honest uncertainty, un-paywalled live truth, and private personal intelligence is open territory precisely because it is where the whole market is weakest.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The market splits into three layers. **Direct competitors** (AllTrails, Komoot, Gaia GPS, onX Backcountry, CalTopo, FarOut, Hiking Project, Outdooractive, Wikiloc, Strava, Garmin, COROS) compete on corpus coverage, navigation, and — increasingly — paywalled live conditions. **Lateral mechanic-donors** (onX Hunt, Citymapper, Flighty, Letterboxd, Oura, WHOOP, Spotify, Beli, Yuka) each ship one transferable interaction primitive worth more than most competitors' whole feature sets. **Anti-patterns** (Duolingo, plus the engagement machinery inside Strava/Garmin/Beli) are the design vocabulary to reject.

Four findings cut across all of them:

1. **Provenance is a feature, never a posture.** FarOut's Water Status (trailing two-week window, ordinal 1–3 average, *silence* when no fresh report) is the only true source-or-silence pattern shipped in the wild. onX's "days since the report" clock and Yuka's worst-element ceiling are the next-cleanest. No app unifies freshness + authority + corroboration into one honestly-hedged confidence property.
2. **Capability ≠ preference is endemic and lucrative.** Garmin, Oura, WHOOP, and COROS are world-class *capability* sensors that all drift into telling you what you *should* do; Strava conflates "how fit you are" with "what you'd enjoy." Outcome-learned *private* preference (Letterboxd/Beli's pairwise model) is nearly unowned in the outdoor space.
3. **Engagement machinery is data-poisoning, not just calm-violating.** Strava crowns, Duolingo streaks, Garmin badges, and Beli leaderboards corrupt the very taste signal a recommender needs by making logging a score-chasing activity. **Anti-engagement is therefore also pro-accuracy.**
4. **The defensible difference is a fusion no incumbent attempts:** per-fact source + timestamp + confidence on a slow corpus *and* JIT overlays, an outcome-learned private taste model that separates capability from preference, and a finite calm surface.

## 3. Defensible white space

What we can own that no incumbent does:

1. **Unified confidence as one honestly-hedged property** (freshness × authority × corroboration) attached to *every* fact — slow-corpus trail geometry *and* every JIT overlay. The whole field shows at most a raw timestamp (onX "days since," Windy issue-time) or nothing, and *none* hedge the presentation when confidence is low.
2. **Source-or-silence as a system invariant**, not a single feature. FarOut ships it for water only; Hiking Project for conditions only (a global ~100-day cliff). Nobody applies *per-data-type* freshness windows (weather in hours, streamflow in minutes, structural data in months) with visible age + last-source across the whole product, and nobody renders absence-of-fresh-data as absence-of-claim system-wide.
3. **A finite, decision-complete verdict layer.** Gaia/CalTopo hand you 6 overlays and make *you* the analyst; Citymapper is a perpetually re-querying dashboard. Nobody synthesizes into "this trip is go / marginal / no-go, and here is exactly which live condition (with its source + timestamp) is the binding constraint," then lets you close the app. *(See the reduction-logic sketch in §8 — this is the report's biggest strategic claim and also its thinnest on "how.")*
4. **Outcome-learned PRIVATE taste that separates capability from preference.** Garmin/Oura/WHOOP/COROS learn capability and never taste; Letterboxd/Beli learn taste but publicly and with no physiological reality-check. The fusion — pairwise-learned private preference + watch readiness used *only* as a non-ranking capability floor — is essentially unowned outdoors.
5. **Capability-calibrated difficulty.** Hiking Project, AllTrails, and Komoot all show *identical* community-average difficulty to every viewer ("Difficulty ratings are an average of all votes"). A difficulty learned from *this* individual's logged outcomes and current readiness — the same trail hedged differently for a strong hiker vs. a recovering one — is open.
6. **Un-paywalled, un-staled live safety truth.** AllTrails paywalls Trail Conditions (currently Plus + Peak, after a Nov-2025 reversal — see §4); Gaia/onX gate live overlays and authoritative boundaries behind premium. A utility that never gates freshness/safety behind a tier and never persists a JIT overlay is a trust position the subscription incumbents structurally can't occupy. *(Economics caveat in §8: incumbents paywall freshness partly because live API calls cost money. This must be a deliberate, cost-controlled position, not an unpriced wish.)*
7. **Conclusion-level (not track-level) private sharing.** onX shares raw pins; Garmin shares a raw route. Nobody shares the *derived conclusion* ("this loop fits your party's pace and today's flows") with its provenance trail while structurally stripping the substrate — and nobody pairs that with Strava-proof residual-metadata auditing.
8. **Anonymous browsing of the world + live conditions with no hardware lock-in.** Garmin/COROS/Oura/WHOOP value is gated behind $200–700 hardware plus, increasingly, a subscription; the readiness incumbents collapse without the device. A watch-free-first planner where watch data is pure enrichment owns the un-locked-in middle.
9. **Discovery that surfaces UNDER-trafficked options.** Heatmaps (Strava/Garmin/AllTrails) and Wikiloc TrailRank all bias toward already-popular lines; Hiking Project's map *literally hides* trails not yet "hiked, commented on, and/or tracked." A taste-and-conditions matcher can recommend currently-good-but-obscure trips. **Caveat (acknowledged tension):** obscure trails are obscure partly because they're unverified, so source-or-silence will systematically *silence* exactly the quiet trails this white-space wants to surface. Resolve by leaning on slow-corpus *structural* facts (which age slowly and stay verifiable) for obscure trails, and disclosing the thin live-condition coverage rather than suppressing the candidate entirely.

### Per-app: the one move + the gap

**AllTrails.** *Steal* the per-segment **forecast play-through** — Trail Conditions animates the forecast across the route's distance and the next hours, so you see what mile 4 looks like at 2pm, not one summary number. It binds a live overlay to specific points in space+time. *Avoid* the paired anti-pattern: gating safety-relevant freshness behind a tier. AllTrails made Trail Conditions Peak-exclusive at launch (a reviewer called it "underhanded"), then *reversed it* in Nov 2025, returning it to Plus and extending Peak 3 months free ([AllTrails Help](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/42701770204820-Membership-updates-Nov-2025)). Note also that Trail Conditions is officially "expected condition information" — a forecast, not "real-time" ([AllTrails PR](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alltrails-expands-membership-offering-with-alltrails-peak-302451541.html)); TechCrunch's "real-time" framing is wrong and should not be repeated. **Gap:** own honest uncertainty, un-paywalled live truth, and private personal intelligence — exactly AllTrails' three weakest points.

**Komoot.** *Steal* the per-route **surface + waytype breakdown bar** (asphalt/gravel/singletrack) paired with a red-on-steep elevation profile and hover-to-locate, made into a *provenance hook*: tap a gravel segment to see when that surface was last verified and from which source ([Komoot route planner](https://www.komoot.com/help/routeplanner)). **Gap:** Komoot shows composition honestly but never tells you whether the gravel is currently washed out, has no live JIT overlay, and personalizes only by sport-segment, never from your own logged outcomes.

**Gaia GPS.** *Steal* the **layered overlay with per-layer opacity slider and per-layer source + cadence label** — render every JIT condition (NWS, USGS streamflow, FIRMS, AirNow, RIDB) as a toggleable, source-stamped overlay on the same canvas, never fused into one opaque score. Gaia is the reference implementation of slow base + JIT overlays, with named agency sources and update cadences ([Gaia wildfire/smoke maps](https://blog.gaiagps.com/wildfire-and-smoke-maps/), [snow depth](https://blog.gaiagps.com/snow-depth-map/)). **Gap:** Gaia fetches JIT but does *not* disclose age at the surface — a 23-hour-old layer renders identically to fresh — and makes *you* the analyst of 6 overlays instead of synthesizing a verdict. (Pricing note: standalone Premium is now ~$59.99/yr, not the older $39.99; Outside+ bundle $89.99/yr.)

**onX Backcountry.** *Steal* the **Trail Report card**: a 3-state status (All Clear / Some Issues / Closed) + absolute date + a computed "reported N days ago" staleness clock, with light attribution (first name + last initial) and zero gamification — *let visible age do the hedging* instead of a prose disclaimer ([onX Trail Report](https://www.onxmaps.com/backcountry/app/features/trail-report)). Apply this exact pattern to every JIT overlay. **Gap:** onX gates authoritative land boundaries behind a $99.99/yr Elite tier and presents them as flat, un-provenanced certainties; it has no taste model and no conclusion-level sharing.

**CalTopo.** *Steal* the **composable, named, savable terrain-condition rule-builder** (slope AND aspect AND elevation AND canopy, painted on the map) — but *invert the surface*: derive the rule from logged outcomes (capability vs. preference) and present it as an explainable, editable overlay instead of hand-authored ([CalTopo custom shading](https://www.alpinesavvy.com/blog/caltopo-pro-tip-custom-terrain-shading)). **Gap:** CalTopo hands you raw query/overlay power and never says "this trip fits you," never learns from outcomes, and links sources without ever flagging staleness.

**FarOut.** *Steal* **Water Status**: ML-score every comment in a trailing 14-day window into an ordinal (1=dry / 2=low / 3=flowing), average it, **show NOTHING when no report exists in the window**, and keep raw dated comments one tap below as auditable substrate ([FarOut Water Status](https://faroutguides.com/new-farout-feature-provides-water-status-at-a-glance/) — empty-state behavior confirmed: "If no comments have been posted within two weeks, no status indicator will be shown"). This is the explicit template for Rule 1. **Gap:** identical for everyone (no personalization), water-only, crowd-only (no USGS/NWS authority cross-check), and public-by-default.

**Hiking Project.** *Steal* the **conditions-staleness auto-expiry**: a condition not refreshed within a window auto-downgrades to an explicit "Unknown" rather than a comforting-but-rotten green check ([Hiking Project help](https://www.hikingproject.com/help/21/overview-of-site-name-features) — confirmed: "If conditions have not been updated in 100 days, the site will report unknown"). *Improve it* to per-data-type windows showing actual age + last source. *Avoid* its leaderboards and public check-ins. **Gap:** one global 100-day cliff, no live JIT overlays, community-average difficulty identical for all, and discovery that hides quiet trails.

**Outdooractive.** *Steal* the **"Current Conditions" expiry + time-slider**: each ephemeral report carries a validity window, auto-demotes to "no longer up-to-date" after 14 days, but stays inspectable up to 365 days behind an "as of" slider — ephemera never rot into trail facts. **Gap:** attribution is by class (tourism-org vs. community), not per-fact source + timestamp; publish-to-commons by default; no private taste. *(No primary-source claims were supplied for Outdooractive's exact behavior in the verification pass; treat the mechanic description as accurate-in-spirit and confirm specifics before building against them.)*

**Wikiloc.** *Steal* the **off-route audio + haptic follow alert** (calm, hands-free, fully offline) — but *re-aim it* from a static anonymous GPX to a live-verified route where each segment carries source + last-verified timestamp and the alert also fires on freshness/condition breaches, not only geographic deviation ([Wikiloc usage](https://travesiapirenaica.com/en/how-to-use-wikiloc/)). **Gap:** a Wikiloc track is one anonymous user's day with no source/freshness; trust is laundered through TrailRank popularity (which penalizes correct-but-obscure trails — the exact ranking penalty Rule 2 forbids); publish-by-default.

**Strava.** *Avoid* **Local Legends** — the crown for most efforts in a rolling 90 days, awarded "regardless of pace or speed," where *only* activities visible to "Everyone" count ([Strava Local Legends](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043099552-Local-Legends)). It converts mere repetition into a defendable status object and weaponizes public exposure as the price of status. The exact mechanic to reject: never turn frequency-of-use into a competitive, defendable, expiring rank requiring public visibility. Also avoid the heatmap re-identification failure (see §7). **Gap (what we own):** the prospective, condition-aware "should I go here, now, with this party?" instead of the retrospective "look what I did."

**Garmin Connect.** *Steal* the **Course-share link**: share the derived plan (route + elevation + categorized climbs) as a self-contained, immediately-actionable artifact while each person's activity history stays private — Rule 4's "share the conclusion, not the substrate" made concrete. *Avoid* the badge/points/levels/streak engine wholesale (~1,000 badges, 10 levels, repeatable streak badges like the 60-Day Goal Getter — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2023/09/04/garwin-the-garmin-badges-calendar-and-database/)). **Gap:** live conditions, place/taste preference, honest hedging instead of confident black-box scores, and no hardware lock-in. Garmin is also the proof-case for Rule 7: Body Battery and Training Readiness are excellent capability sensors that learn *zero* place preference.

**COROS.** *Steal* the **read-only, conclusion-only data seam**: expose derived recovery/readiness *conclusions* over an authorized, scoped boundary while withholding raw per-second HR/GPS substrate — consume the conclusion as enrichment (degrade-and-disclose), never depend on it. **Hedge:** at its May-2026 launch, the official COROS MCP was read-only with 15 endpoints and deliberately withheld raw streams — but the official MCP page (updated June 2026) now advertises *optional write access* and exposure of `.fit` files with full GPS tracks and second-by-second data ([COROS MCP](https://coros.com/stories/coros-metrics/c/mcp-testing)). Treat "read-only, conclusion-only" as the *design stance we want*, not a permanent property of the COROS product; verify the current contract before architecting against it. **Gap:** COROS only follows GPX you already chose — no discovery, no taste, no provenance/hedging, no live world conditions.

## 5. Lens B — Lateral mechanics

Each lateral app donates one primitive. Mapped to the precise invariant it serves.

**onX Hunt → Rule 4 (private-by-default, share-by-exception).** **Folder-scoped, two-tier (View-only / Contribute), revocable, no-re-share sharing.** The folder is the unit of disclosure; the permission tier bounds the flow; re-sharing is structurally blocked; unshare *deletes the recipient's copy while preserving the owner's*. Recipients "only see what you add to the Folder," never your other private content ([onX Hunt folders](https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/app/features/collaborative-folders), [sharing markups](https://support.onxmaps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003218311-Sharing-Markups-with-other-onX-Hunt-users)). This is the concrete UX of grant-as-stop-point on a provenance edge.

**Citymapper → Rule 3 (slow geometry + JIT overlay) + Rule 6.** **Opt-in per-entity "subscribe to this line" alerts** that fire only when the watched entity's status crosses a threshold *and state the derived consequence* ("here are the adjusted best routes"), not a raw status string ([Citymapper line alerts](https://citymapper.com/news/1454/get-notified-when-your-line-is-disrupted)). Pair it with Citymapper's other steal: render every condition with an explicit live-vs-stale visual state so a fallback/cached value is never mistaken for fresh. Transfer directly: subscribe to a trail/trailhead/permit/river gauge; get a calm push only when *that* overlay crosses a cutoff.

**Flighty → Rule 1 + Rule 6.** The **25-hour "Where's My Plane" inbound-substrate watch** — instead of waiting for the official party (the airline) to declare a problem, Flighty watches the *upstream causal substrate* (the specific inbound aircraft, ATC/airspace state) and pushes one calm, timestamped "heads-up, here's why" alert *ahead* of the official source, naming the specific cause. Transfer: pre-compute the leading indicator of a disruption from primary data (a streamflow trend, a fire-perimeter advance) and beat the "official" word. The user sees one clear notification with the reason, not a dashboard. *(Note: Flighty's "Passport" stat-ledger is "optimized for sharing on social media" — that's the anti-pattern edge to leave behind.)*

**Letterboxd → Rule 5 (taste from lightweight logged outcomes).** The **"fade out what you've already logged" rendering rule**: show the full ranked slate but dim trips the user already logged (with a faint "you hiked this · liked it"), so the eye is pulled to the unseen while history stays as context. Zero extra input, no "recommended for you" carousel, no scoring banner — passive personalization that emerges from the diary itself ([Nanocrowd/Letterboxd](https://nanocrowd.com/nanocrowd-letterboxd/)). Letterboxd also models the clean separation of *rating* (quality), *like* (affinity), and *rewatch* (would-repeat) as distinct signals worth logging separately.

**Oura → Rule 2 (confidence via drill-down) + Rule 5 (capability sensor).** The **single composite that EXPANDS into a contributor breakdown of plain-language questions**, judged *baseline-relative* (against the user's own history), with a **Rest Mode that visibly re-weights and TELLS the user it changed** ("emphasizing recovery, deprioritizing activity") rather than silently re-ranking ([Oura Readiness contributors](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533-Readiness-Contributors), [Rest Mode](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057065433-Rest-Mode)). *Avoid* collapsing thin-data into one falsely-crisp number with no per-input confidence. *(Correction to respect: Oura's "Balance" contributors don't all use one ~2-month baseline — HRV Balance compares against a 3-month average.)*

**WHOOP → Rule 2 (evidence floor) + Rule 5 (within-person learning).** The **Journal behavior-correlation with a hard sample-size gate** — contrast outcomes on days-with vs. days-without a behavior, and only surface a claimed effect once a minimum paired threshold is met (WHOOP gates its Monthly Performance Assessment at ≥5 "yes" *and* ≥5 "no" within 90 days, plus 14 recorded recoveries — [WHOOP monthly assessment](https://www.whoop.com/eu/en/thelocker/monthly-performance-assessment/)), delivered in a finite periodic digest. Transfer: "On the 6 trips you started before 7am you finished strong; on the 5 late starts you reported being gassed — early starts correlate with better outcomes for you (n=11)." *Avoid* WHOOP's companion failure: a single precise Recovery % presented with no confidence/freshness hedge. *(Treat the exact 5/5/14 numbers as an illustrative floor; the principle, not the spec, is what transfers.)*

**Spotify Discover Weekly → Rule 6 (calm finite ritual).** The **fixed-size, fixed-cadence "replace-don't-accumulate" drop** — ~N items on a known cadence that *overwrites* the prior set. Finiteness IS the product: the user knows there's a bottom, a calm heartbeat without streaks or infinite scroll ([Spotify 10-year post](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-06-30/discover-weekly-turns-10-celebrating-100-billion-tracks-streamed-and-a-decade-of-personalized-discovery/)). Let it degrade to *fewer flagged* candidates when live data is thin. **Hedge:** the claim that finiteness *specifically* drives retention is an inference — Spotify also runs heavy engagement machinery around the playlist, so attribute the calm pattern to the mechanic's *shape*, not to a proven retention causation. **Quantitative caveat (acknowledged gap):** Spotify ships exactly 30 regardless of certainty; a trip has far higher per-item cost (a day of driving vs. 3 minutes), so N for trips should be small (think single digits) and *driven by what's verifiable*, never a fixed quota.

**Beli → Rule 5 (high-signal preference) + Rule 4 (private ordinal, share only the conclusion).** **Pairwise-comparison ranking decoupled from gamification**: coarse 3-bucket sentiment (loved / fine / regretted), then ~3–4 binary "better than X?" questions via binary-insertion-sort (worst case ⌊log2(n)+1⌋), interpolated to a 0–10 *derived* score the user never types, with a "too hard to compare" escape ([Beli mechanics](https://notes.ansonbiggs.com/rating-has-never-been-so-good/)). This beats star-clustering: humans can't reliably assign interpretable absolute scores but *can* reliably say A > B. *Reject* the streaks / leaderboards / $0.99 streak-restore IAP that Beli wraps around it.

**Yuka → Rule 2 (hazard ceiling + per-component source).** **Transparent weighted-decomposition score with a WORST-ELEMENT CEILING**: a headline number always expands into named weighted sub-factors with tappable source links, and a single high-risk component *caps* the whole result regardless of how good everything else is — no averaging-away a safety flag ([Yuka food scoring](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/ijzgfvi1jq-how-are-food-products-scored), [cosmetic penalties](https://help.yuka.io/l/en/article/ih5pet4ffc-how-are-penalties-calculated-in-the-cosmetic-product-scores)). This is the precise analog of "a single critical hazard caps the safety verdict but confidence never penalizes desirability ranking." **Gap to fill:** Yuka has no freshness timestamp or confidence band — fine for a slow nutrition label, fatal for weather/streamflow/permits. Add the freshness/confidence Yuka lacks.

## 6. Anti-pattern watch — explicit "do not build" rules

**Duolingo (the canonical anti-pattern).** *Streak-as-loss-aversion*: a return-or-lose counter shown first on open, reset to zero on a missed day, with paid streak-repair monetizing the manufactured anxiety. The server stores `current_streak_count` + `last_completed_date` + timezone and zeroes on a miss; the design intent is that users return because they're "terrified of seeing that counter reset to zero" ([apptitude](https://apptitude.io/blog/how-duolingos-streak-mechanic-actually-works/)). Layered on top: weekly Leagues (30-person XP leaderboards with promotion/demotion), anthropomorphized guilt notifications (a crying owl, "Duo misses you"), and the manipulative "we'll stop sending reminders" copy that itself pulls users back.

**Do-not-build rules, drawn from across the anti-patterns:**

- **No streaks or consecutive-day counters.** (Duolingo, Snapchat, Apple Watch rings.) Make value PERSIST losslessly — the durable taste model and outcome log are durable private assets, not a flame that dies at midnight.
- **No leaderboards, crowns, kudos, or approval metrics.** (Strava Local Legends/KOMs — which incentivized cheating at scale, ~2.3M e-bike + ~1.6M vehicle activities removed, and physically dangerous riding; Garmin badges/levels; Beli city/global leaderboards.)
- **No paid relief from manufactured anxiety.** (Duolingo streak repair, Beli $0.99 streak-restore.) Never sell escape from a fear the product created.
- **No anthropomorphized / guilt / "we'll stop messaging you" notifications.** Notify *only* on a live, source-backed, time-sensitive reason (a permit window opens, a fire closure lifts) — which the JIT architecture naturally supports.
- **No infinite scroll, no for-you dwell-optimized feed.** A finite feed that ends. Letterboxd's chronological/follow-based discovery and Spotify's bounded weekly drop are the calm models.
- **No fabricated affect posing as feedback.** (Duolingo's mascot mood substituting for an honest mastery signal.) This is a Rule-1 violation — an emotional state with no honest basis.
- **Never surface readiness AS A SCORE / ring / target / streak.** The wearable-anxiety literature is the hard warning: a capability sensor turned into a daily goal is a documented preference-distorting stressor (deflated step counts measurably lowered self-esteem and raised blood pressure/heart rate; calorie-tracking devices linked to disordered-eating traits — [Fortune, Jan 2025](https://fortune.com/well/2025/01/24/apple-watch-bullied-burn-calories-close-rings-obsession-fitness-trackers-notifications/)).

**Attribution hedge (respect this).** The "Apple Watch backlash" is best stated as the *general wearable-readiness-anxiety / orthosomnia literature*, not an Apple-specific shipped-readiness-score event — Apple Watch ships no native readiness/"Vitality" score (Vitals is a metrics view; "Vitality" is rumored/unreleased). The Fortune piece documents ring-closing compulsion (late-night walking, pacing) but *not* the "jumping jacks during a movie" anecdote, which is a separate personal blog. Keep the warning; fix the brand specificity.

**The meta-rule.** Anti-engagement is also *pro-accuracy*. Gamified logging (Untappd badges, Beli leaderboards) doesn't just break calm — it *poisons the taste signal* by making logging a score-chasing activity rather than honest outcome capture. Untappd's aggregate ratings systematically over-score high-ABV/hyped styles, are produced by <1% of drinkers, and are still used as authoritative gatekeeping ([Good Beer Hunting](https://www.goodbeerhunting.com/sightlines/2021/2/23/how-untappd-ratings-became-craft-beers-most-fickle-prize)) — the canonical "star-clustering + selection-bias-as-fact" failure that pairwise capture (Beli) and private logging are designed to avoid. So **the only legitimate logging incentive is durable, lossless value** — operationalized as a designed loop (see §8), not a slogan.

## 7. Invariant-by-invariant map

**Rule 1 — Source-or-silence.** *Best exemplars:* FarOut (the only true silence-when-stale shipped); Hiking Project (~100-day → "Unknown"); Flighty (every alert traces to a named authoritative substrate, beats the official word); Yuka (tappable per-component source links). *Anti-models:* AllTrails/Gaia/Strava/onX-boundaries/Wikiloc all show undated, unsourced data as ambient truth. *Design move:* make **absence of a fresh source render as absence of a claim**, never as a stale-but-styled-normal value — adopt FarOut's silence rule as the explicit template. Paint fetched-at + named source on every overlay value *and* on the slow corpus; when a live call fails, flag it, never fabricate.

> **Silence-UX addition (the hardest consequence of this rule).** An honest app in a data-sparse region risks reading as broken/empty. The surface must visibly *distinguish* four states: **no data** ("no source covers this here"), **no hazard** ("checked, clear"), **not-yet-fetched** ("tap to verify / fetching"), and **stale-and-degraded** ("last known 3 days ago, may have changed"). Same-looking gray for all four is the failure. Design these four as distinct, legible affordances before shipping the verdict layer.

**Rule 2 — Confidence as one property (freshness · authority · corroboration).** *Best exemplars:* Yuka (worst-element ceiling — one high-risk component caps the verdict instead of being averaged away); Oura (single number → sourced contributor breakdown); WHOOP (≥5/≥5 evidence floor before a correlation claim); onX (days-since clock); Windy (per-layer model issue-time). *No competitor unifies freshness + authority + corroboration into one honestly-hedged property.* *Design move:* combine age + source authority + cross-report corroboration into ONE hedged confidence that hedges the *presentation* when low. Borrow Yuka's hazard ceiling (a flash-flood streamflow or AQI hazard caps the surfaced safety verdict regardless of scenery, while confidence never penalizes desirability ranking), WHOOP's sample-size gate before asserting any learned correlation, and Oura's drill-down so a headline always decomposes into sourced sub-factors.

> **Source-conflict addition.** "Corroboration" is named as an input but *contradiction* is unhandled. Define the behavior when two *fresh* sources disagree (NWS says clear; a 2-hour-old on-the-ground report says flooded): for the *safety ceiling*, a recent on-the-ground report should override a model forecast (recency + ground-truth authority wins); corroboration confidence *drops* and the presentation hedges ("conflicting reports — treating as unsafe pending verification"). Never silently average a disagreement into a comfortable middle.

**Rule 3 — Slow corpus + JIT overlays.** *Best exemplars:* Gaia GPS & CalTopo (slow topo/DEM base + live weather/fire/snow/streamflow overlays, never baked into base tiles); Citymapper (static line/stop geometry + JIT arrivals — the cleanest urban analog); Outdooractive (auto-expiring conditions + 365-day "as of" slider); Flighty (JIT inbound watch over a slow itinerary). *Validated but flawed:* all fetch JIT yet *don't disclose age at the surface*. *Design move:* the architectural split is proven; the gap is **surface disclosure**. Paint fetched-at + next-refresh on every live overlay and visibly degrade (gray/hedge) a cached overlay past its window. **Readiness/Body-Battery is itself fast/ephemeral data — treat it as a JIT overlay fetched at planning time, never persisted as a graph node to stale.** Steal Outdooractive's "as of" slider so prior states stay inspectable without polluting the present.

> **Offline/degraded-connectivity addition.** Backcountry use is the actual context, yet "fetched-at + next-refresh + silence-when-stale" assumes connectivity at decision time. Generalize Wikiloc's offline strength into the freshness model: on-trail with no signal, show **last-known-good with a prominent age stamp and a "cannot verify now" flag** rather than either silence (which hides useful cached info) or false-fresh (which lies). The pre-trip plan should snapshot the freshest verified state and carry forward an honest "as of departure" label.

**Rule 4 — Private-by-default, share-by-exception.** *Best exemplars:* onX Hunt (owner-private by default, view-only/contribute folder grants, no re-share, revocable unshare that deletes the recipient's copy); Find My (per-recipient, time-boxed grants — One Hour / Until End of Day / Indefinitely — with *silent* revocation); Garmin/COROS (share the derived route/recovery-conclusion, keep substrate private). *Cautionary tale:* **Strava redacted the conclusion (home/trailhead) but leaked it via residual substrate.** *Design move:* adopt onX's grant model as baseline and **enforce it at the query/data (Cypher) layer, never in the agent.** Audit every shared payload for residual metadata (distances spanning hidden segments, source edges, co-traveler links, raw track geometry) and strip the substrate *before* emit — never rely on UI redaction. Make grants time-boxed and per-recipient by default, with silent revocation (a safety property for household members). Aggregation is not anonymity — enforce the k-anonymity floor as a hard threshold AND perturb per-user contribution so a heavy logger can't be singled out.

> **Privacy-claim re-attribution (respect this).** Split the Strava story into two separately-sourced findings: (a) the **verified** NC State CONPRO-2023 heatmap de-anonymization — ~37.5% accuracy for highly active users (~308 activities), because more activity = more heat = more identifiable ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/strava-heatmap-feature-can-be-abused-to-find-home-addresses/)); and (b) the KU Leuven privacy-zone attack that recovered up to **85% of protected home locations** by exploiting the *total-distance metadata* that still includes the segment ridden inside the hidden zone ([inkl/KU Leuven](https://www.inkl.com/news/hackers-can-find-your-home-on-strava-even-if-you-use-privacy-settings-researchers-find)). These are *different attacks*; do not fuse them into one "85% heatmap" claim. Both reinforce the same lesson: hiding the conclusion is insufficient if the substrate leaks it.

**Rule 5 — Outcome + watch personalization, where capability ≠ preference.** *Best exemplars:* Garmin/Oura/WHOOP/COROS (world-class capability sensors — Body Battery, Readiness, Recovery — that all drift into a should/verdict); Letterboxd & Beli (the *preference* model: taste from lightweight logged outcomes, pairwise ranking that beats star-clustering, separable rating/like/rewatch signals); Spotify (taste from passive skips/completions). The implicit-feedback literature formalizes the hazard: a completed hike or good readiness score proves *feasibility*, never *desire* — a large fraction of implicit positives are false positives (curiosity, position/title bias — [Denoising Implicit Feedback, WSDM'21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04153)). *Design move:* keep watch-derived readiness strictly on the **capability axis** — a soft effort floor that degrades-and-discloses, never driving taste/recommendation ranking. Learn *preference* only from explicit logged outcomes via Beli's pairwise comparison, storing the private ordinal as substrate and any 0–10 as a derived presentation artifact.

> **Cold-start + anti-spam addition.** Beli's binary-insertion-sort needs an existing ranked list to insert against; the first 0–5 logged trips have nothing to compare to — exactly when a new user most needs recommendations. *Bootstrap:* for the first N trips, lean on conditions + the capability floor + slow-corpus content features (terrain/distance/exposure) before taste exists, and only start asking pairwise questions once there's a list to insert into. *Anti-spam:* a single fresh UGC report must not flip a safety verdict — require corroboration or trust-weight reporters before one report moves a safety flag (the Yuka-ceiling logic should demand more than n=1 to *raise* a hazard from an unverified single source).
>
> **Capability/preference decomposition addition.** A single outcome ("I bailed at mile 6") is *both* a capability and a preference signal. The separation is achievable but must be designed, not assumed: capture *why* at log time as a coarse, optional tag ("too hard physically" → capability; "didn't enjoy it / weather miserable / boring" → preference), and let the pairwise comparison carry preference while the bail-point + readiness-at-the-time carry capability. Don't infer one axis from the other.
>
> **Party/household composition addition (Epic 007 flags this as unspecified).** The product is household-planning, but readiness is per-individual. A green-and-a-red readiness pair must compose into one party effort floor — adopt **weakest-link with per-member disclosure** ("party floor set by [member], recovering"), not a hidden min or an average that erases the constraint.

**Rule 6 — Calm, finite, anti-engagement.** *Best exemplars:* Spotify Discover Weekly (finite N-item, replace-don't-accumulate, predictable cadence); Gaia/CalTopo/COROS/onX Hunt/Flighty (commercially-viable calm utilities); Citymapper (task-bounded GO nav + opt-in alerts). *Anti-models:* Duolingo, Strava, Garmin, Beli (see §6). Calm-tech (Amber Case) and Humane Tech (CHT) supply ready guardrails. *Design move:* adopt Amber Case's calm-tech principles as *testable* guardrails — no streaks, no leaderboards, no kudos, no infinite scroll, a finite feed that ends. State the success metric as a **non-engagement** one ("trips taken the user rated good"; "user got the answer and closed the app") — never DAU or session length. Notifications only on a live, source-backed, time-sensitive reason. Make accumulated context a durable, lossless private asset — the inverse of Duolingo's flame.

> **Notification rate-limiting addition.** "One calm push when a threshold crosses" becomes a flood if a user subscribes to 10 entities during a volatile-weather window — precisely when alerts matter most. Add a debounce + per-event cap + digest-rollup: collapse multiple simultaneous threshold-crossings into one consolidated "3 of your watched trips are now affected — tap for details" push, never N separate buzzes.

## 8. Tensions & risks

Where a tempting mechanic collides with an invariant.

**T1 — Taste-personalization vs. source-or-silence (Rule 5 vs. 1/2/7).** A learned preference is an *inference*; surfacing it as a stated fact replicates exactly what Oura/WHOOP/COROS get wrong (a categorical verdict posing as measured truth). *Mitigation:* every user-belief carries provenance + confidence + timestamp; the pairwise-derived 0–10 is a derived presentation artifact, never persisted as a fact; a thin-data taste call must *read* as hedged, not crisp.

**T2 — Watch-readiness enrichment vs. capability ≠ preference (Rule 7 vs. 5).** The strongest pull is to let a green readiness score imply you *should* take a hard trip — the precise Garmin/Strava conflation. *Mitigation:* readiness gates *effort* (a non-ranking soft floor) and never picks or ranks the trip; it degrades-and-discloses and always defers to felt state and logged outcomes.

**T3 — Any social/sharing or commons mechanic vs. anti-engagement + privacy (Rules 4/8 vs. 6).** Even benign sharing (onX folders, Spotify-style drops) can slide into a feed, a follow graph, or a status object; the commons fork risks Strava's two failures (aggregation ≠ anonymity; the most-active user is most-identifiable). *Mitigation:* sharing is a utility action (a revocable grant, a link), never a feed; enforce k-anonymity as a hard floor; perturb/cap per-user contribution; strip residual substrate before any emit.

**T4 — Finite curated drop vs. source-or-silence (Rule 6 vs. 1).** A fixed-N weekly quota tempts padding the list with low-confidence candidates to hit the count (Spotify always fills 30 regardless of certainty). *Resolution:* favor source-or-silence — let the drop shrink to fewer clearly-flagged candidates when live data is thin. **Finiteness is driven by what's verifiable, never by a quota.**

**T5 — Under-trafficked discovery vs. source-or-silence (white-space #9 vs. Rule 1).** Quiet trails are quiet partly because they're unverified, so silence-when-stale will systematically suppress the very trips the discovery white-space wants to surface. *Resolution:* for obscure candidates, rank on slow-corpus *structural* facts (which age slowly and stay verifiable) and *disclose* thin live coverage ("structurally promising; few recent reports") rather than hiding the candidate.

**T6 — The logging-incentive meta-tension (Rule 5 vs. 6).** Any mechanic that increases logging helps the taste model but, if gamified (Beli leaderboards, Untappd badges), poisons the signal it feeds *and* breaks calm. *Resolution — operationalize "durable lossless value" as a designed loop, not a slogan:* the outcome log visibly **sharpens the next finite drop and the private taste ranking**, shown as accreting personal context ("your last 8 logged trips have tightened these recommendations"). The reward for logging is a *better answer next time*, surfaced at the moment of the next drop — never a streak, rank, or score.

**R1 — The verdict-layer reduction is under-specified (white-space #3).** "Go / marginal / no-go and here's the binding constraint" is the biggest strategic claim and the thinnest on *how*. Sketch of the reduction: each live overlay maps to a per-data-type **threshold function** producing a per-condition state (safe / marginal / unsafe) + a confidence; the trip verdict is the **worst per-condition state** (Yuka ceiling — no averaging-away a hazard); the **binding constraint** is the single condition that produced that worst state, surfaced with its source + timestamp + confidence. Crucially this reconciles with Rule 2: the *safety verdict* is a ceiling/floor judgment, separate from the *desirability ranking* (taste + novelty + scenery), which confidence never penalizes. Build the threshold functions per data type before claiming the verdict layer ships.

**R2 — Un-paywalled-live-truth economics (weak spot).** Incumbents paywall freshness because live API calls cost money — the obvious reason no one occupies this space (it may be uneconomic, not unimagined). *Stance to make explicit:* fund it via JIT caching, per-data-type refresh windows as a deliberate cost lever (weather hourly, structural data monthly — which the architecture needs anyway), and region-scoped pilots. The moat must read as a *deliberate sustainable position*, not an unpriced wish.

**R3 — Counter-argument to total anti-engagement (weak spot).** The calm-tech canon treats all structure as poison, but *some* cadence (a reason to return) is what makes a tool sticky enough to accrue the logged-outcome data the taste model needs. The distinction to hold: a **calm cadence** (Spotify's predictable, lossless, no-FOMO weekly drop) is legitimate; **manipulation** (loss-aversion, variable reward, manufactured scarcity) is not. The finite weekly drop is the permitted cadence; everything in §6 is the forbidden manipulation.
